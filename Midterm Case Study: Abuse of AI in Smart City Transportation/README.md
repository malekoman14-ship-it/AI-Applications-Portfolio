This case study analyzes the abuse and ethical risks of AI in smart city transportation systems, 
focusing on how data-driven technologies can be misused. While AI improves traffic management, safety,
and efficiency, it also introduces serious concerns related to privacy, surveillance, and bias. In 
systems like Singapore’s intelligent transport network, large-scale data collection through sensors 
and cameras can track individual movement patterns, raising concerns about consent and misuse of 
personal data.

Key issues include surveillance creep, where infrastructure designed for traffic control may be used 
for monitoring citizens, and algorithmic bias that may favor certain communities over others. 
Additionally, the digital divide can exclude vulnerable populations, while automation threatens 
traditional jobs.

This analysis highlights the need for ethical AI governance, transparency, and fair data practices to 
ensure AI benefits society without compromising individual rights and social equity.
